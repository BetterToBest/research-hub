C23 · Drug policy summary: two figures retired

research-summary.html · drug-policy-reform.html (paper body still to be aligned)

Said: $1.5 trillion in prohibition costs versus $847 billion in potential savings over 25 years.

Now: a cost-benefit framework comparing prohibition with regulatory alternatives, using the paper's own cost estimates.

Why: the paper's cost table sums to $4,616B, and $1.5 trillion matches none of its totals. $847B is one line (health cost reductions) of one scenario; the paper's own net benefit is $3,870B (traditional regulation) and $5,044B (integrated).

research-summary.html

C25 · Development paper: "ROI of $1,847" corrected

research-summary.html · community-monetary-development.html (abstract, meta tags, §4.2, conclusion)

Said: a superior ROI of $1,847 per beneficiary; poverty below 5% within 15 years (the summary).

Now: an illustrative net present value of about $2,046 per beneficiary under assumed benefits; poverty converging to a steady state of about 7–12% under assumed rates (the paper's own proposition, corrected Oct 29).

Why: $1,847 is an NPV, not an ROI, and the paper's formula gives $2,046. The $180 a year growing 10% is an assumption. The paper's proof shows poverty settling at 11.8% (7.4% with PTF), never reaching 5%.

C26 · PTH financing summary: $85,000 retired

research-summary.html · pth-independent-financing.html (paper body still to be aligned)

Said: $85,000 in interest savings; sustainability demonstrated at 30–40 households.

Now: the paper's assumed break-even thresholds for each financing model (30–40 households for pay-in-focused trusts).

Why: at the example's own payments, paying the highest rate first saves about $900 and takes 18.3 years. The 30–40 comes from a table of assumed values.
