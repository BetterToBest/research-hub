Nov 26, 2026: the simulation's figures move to its release v5.9.23

The simulation figures on five pages now come from the release · the history page's model figures rerun

dual-currency-inflation.html · cco-ptf-integrated-framework.html · economic-modeling-simulation.html · optimal-transfer-design.html · us-policy-eras.html · index.html · research-summary.html (PDFs rebuilt for the four papers)

What the release is: the simulation's version v5.9.23 (engine 50bc51d). Its main row differs from v5.9.1's in three ways, in both runs of each pair: the lower price at which privately owned essential-service businesses take the programme's currency is set at the sale, as the design has it (the engine had applied it a year late); pay differs by household type; and the child and dependent care credit is included. Every study the pages draw on was rerun on it, under a preregistered amendment written before the reruns, and checked before any figure was read. The simulation's report on the release says what was rerun and checked: dev/reports/s93-release.md.

Said (v5.9.1): programme inflation at full backing 0.00 to 0.18 points a year; at the model's own backing mechanism, which backs 0.2 to 19.6% of the payout, 14.9 to 53.3 points a year; under 5% a year only above about 74-90% backing. The opt-in design at full backing cut basket poverty from 36.1% to 15.2% (20.9 points, 95% interval 20.8 to 21.0) and wealth poverty from 24.6% to 13.7% (10.9 points), and raised median real wealth by about $99,400.

Now says (v5.9.23): 0.00 to 0.19 points a year at full backing; 15.5 to 50.3 points a year at the mechanism, which backs 0.2 to 19.5%; under 5% a year only above about 75-90% backing. The opt-in design cuts basket poverty from 36.4% to 16.0% (20.3 points, 20.2 to 20.5) and wealth poverty from 25.1% to 14.3% (10.8 points), and raises median real wealth by about $99,700. These are generated blocks, rebuilt from the release's data files (tools/build_figures.py).

The history page (US policy eras): its model figures are rerun on the release (results s75-eras-50bc51d). One finding changed: the full-coverage design now slows recovery from an income shock in every era (by 0.22-0.83 years), where before it sped recovery in 17 of 24 cells. The central-bank study, rerun on the same release, finds the same, so the count of cells keeping the direction of that study's first run falls from 227 to 210 of 240, with 30 reversals (6 favourable, 24 not) against 12. The other model figures move a little and each is listed in the page's dated note; the descriptive record is not affected. The page's settings table is now built from the results file instead of typed. Nov 28, 2026: the results repository is offline (GitHub disabled it); the deposit is DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24615619, and every figure can be reproduced from the engine commit named.

Also: the note on the synergy coefficient θ on the front page and the research summary names v5.9.23 (the simulation still gates θ on its zone-coherence setting, checked in the release engine).

Why: the release replaced v5.9.1 as the simulation's current version. The v5.9 figures stay published as the record in the simulation's results repository.

C25 · Development paper: "ROI of $1,847" corrected

research-summary.html · community-monetary-development.html (abstract, meta tags, §4.2, conclusion)

Said: a superior ROI of $1,847 per beneficiary; poverty below 5% within 15 years (the summary).

Now: an illustrative net present value of about $2,046 per beneficiary under assumed benefits; poverty converging to a steady state of about 7–12% under assumed rates (the paper's own proposition, corrected Oct 29).
